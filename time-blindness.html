If you've ever surfaced from deep focus to find your meeting started ten minutes ago — not because you didn't care, but because time slipped — Heads Up is built for exactly that.

Time blindness is real, and it's not a character flaw

Time blindness is difficulty sensing time passing — how much has gone by, how much is left. It's common with ADHD, and it doesn't respond to being told to "just pay attention." When you're absorbed in something, a calendar notification slides in and slides away, and the part of your brain that would've acted on it never got the message.

The usual advice — more alarms, more sticky notes, more calendars — piles on more things that are just as easy to tune out. The problem was never that you needed another gentle nudge.

A reminder you can't tune out

Heads Up does two things that matter when time doesn't register on its own:

  • It shows time passing, visually. A ring in your menu bar fills as your next meeting gets closer — an external clock for a sense of time that isn't always there internally. You can glance and just know, without doing the math.
  • It takes over the whole screen the moment the meeting starts. Not a banner in the corner you'll swipe away on reflex — your entire screen, until you join, snooze, or dismiss. It's genuinely hard to miss, which is the whole point.

It finds the Zoom, Meet, Teams, or Webex link in the invite, so joining is one click — no scrambling through the calendar while you're already late and flustered. And you choose how much warning you get: fifteen minutes out, or two, or right at the start.

Set it once, then stop managing it

There's no system to maintain — the thing that quietly kills most reminder setups. You grant calendar access once, and it watches every calendar you already use (iCloud, Google, Outlook, Exchange). It filters out all-day events, declined invites, and "free" blocks on its own, so it only speaks up for meetings that actually matter.

Private, and paid once

Heads Up reads your calendar entirely on your Mac — no account, no sign-in, no tracking, and nothing leaves your device. And it's a one-time $19.99 purchase, not a subscription: something you're relying on to show up shouldn't add one more monthly bill to keep track of.
